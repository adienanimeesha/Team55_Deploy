*Person 5 & Person 6*

**Marcus:** It would be fun if we record our conversation. Thank you so much. Let me start first. Can you tell me, are you domestic students or international students?
**P5 & P6:** We are both domestic students.

**Marcus:** Could you walk me through what you do when you are between classes and unsure if you will make it on time? Do you open your phone or use an application to navigate?
**P5:** Not now, but we used to. In our first year, we used Google Maps to find where our classes were and where the buildings were.
**P6:** I also use the online university map. It shows the building numbers and where they are. I look at my timetable, check the building number, and then walk there.

**Eric:** Has Google Maps, Apple Maps, or any other navigation app ever given you an inaccurate travel time on campus? What happened, and why do you think it was inaccurate?
**P5:** I think most of the time Google Maps does not really know the building numbers. It knows the big buildings, but not some of the smaller buildings.
**P6:** Yes, for smaller buildings, it often does not know where they are.
**P5 & P6:** The travel time can be wrong because the app does not know the exact location or gives incorrect routes.

**Marcus:** If we are building something that tells you whether you will make it between classes before you start walking, does that sound like something you would use?
**P5 & P6:** Yeah, 100%.
**Eric:** What would make you open this instead of Google Maps or any other navigation app?
**P5:** If I knew it was optimized for university, then I would open it. If something like this existed in our first year, it would have made our life much easier. 
  In first year, we had no idea where anything was. Now we know most of our classes are in similar places, but sometimes we still have classes in buildings we have never been to.
  If it was made for university students, I would use it over Google Maps because I would expect it to be more accurate for campus.
**P6:** I would also use it if it tells me which entrance to take. In some buildings, entering from different sides puts you on different levels.
  For example, if my class is on level two, it is better to know which entrance takes me directly to level two instead of entering from level one and taking a lift.

**Eric:** Would you check it the night before, right before you leave, or at some other time?
**P5:** I would probably check it after I arrive and park my car, or maybe the night before.
**P6:** I would probably check it about ten minutes before class, just before I leave. It would be similar to how people use Google Maps.

**Marcus:** Even in weeks where you already know your routes well, do you think something like this would still be useful, or only at the start of the semester?
**P5:** For engineering students, we have practical classes and labs, and they are not always in the same room. I would use it in those situations.
  But after the first couple of weeks, if I already knew where everything was, I probably would not use it that much.
**P6:** If it had other information, like where all the bathrooms are, that would be useful throughout the semester.

**Marcus:** Now we want to show you our prototype. This is one of my classmates’ timetables.
**P5:** That is sick. That is pretty cool. That is actually really nice. Good job. Is someone going to make this as well? Please, we beg.
**P6:** That is really cool.
**Marcus:** This is the campus route, and this is the indoor navigation feature.
**P5:** That is actually even better. I would actually use the indoor feature.
**P6:** Yes, the indoor feature is probably even better.
**P5 & P6:** Google Maps can get you to the outside of the building, but once you are inside, you have to figure it out yourself.

**Eric:** Do you have any ideas about adding something new to this application?
**P5:** I would add all the bathrooms, because sometimes I cannot find bathrooms.
**P6:** Yes, it should tell me where the nearest bathroom is. It could also show bubblers or places to drink water.
**P5:** It should also include food places, like GYG, sushi, and Subway. It could become a comprehensive campus map.

**Marcus:** If you could choose one priority feature for our application, what would it be?
**P5:** The indoor one.
**P6:** Yes, the indoor one.
**P5:** After first year, you usually know where most buildings are, but you do not always know where the classrooms are inside.
**P6:** Yes, we do not really know which room is where indoors.
**P5:** There was one time I had an assessed practical and I had never been to that classroom before. I almost missed it because I could not find the room. I was running around different levels.
**P6:** So the indoor navigation feature would be very useful.
