### Person 3

**Adiena:** Okay, so before we start, are you an international student or domestic, and what year are you in?

**Person 3:** I'm an international student. It's my third year in uni in general, but first year here at UQ.

**Adiena:** Okay, so walk me through what you do when you're between classes and you're unsure if you'll make it on time. Do you open your phone? And if you do, what do you look at on your phone?

**Person 3:** Yeah, of course I open my phone. I open Google Maps and just check how long it'll take me to the class. And I don't really mind if I'm late because they don't really care about that.

**Adiena:** So basically your main navigation app is Google Maps?

**Person 3:** Mhm.

**Adiena:** Has Google Maps, Apple Maps, or any other navigation application ever given you an accurate travel time on campus?

**Person 3:** Yeah, because I feel like Google Maps, they don't give you the quickest approach because sometimes they don't know that there's like shortcuts and whatnot. And sometimes if I want the time to be accurate, I will check UQ Maps.

**Adiena:** Oh, okay. How often do you check UQ Maps, though?

**Person 3:** I used to check it often in my first month here, but now I already know all the shortcuts so I don't check it anymore.

**Adiena:** So we are building something that tells you whether you will make it between classes before you start walking.Does that sound like something you would use?

**Person 3:** Yeah.

**Adiena:** Since you answered yes, what would make you open this application instead of Google Maps or UQ Maps?

**Person 3:** I think it's if you get the best of both worlds. So you get the shortcuts of UQ Maps, but the navigation clarity of Google Maps. Because UQ Maps, they don't move with you.

**Adiena:** Oh, they don't move with you? What do you mean?

**Person 3:** So, when you're using Google Maps, when you move, it shortens the time and tells you where you already are and whatnot. But UQ Maps doesn't do that. They only give you a starting and an ending point.

**Adiena:** Oh, I see.

**Adiena:** So, if you were to have this application, when would you check it? Would it be the night before, right before you leave, or sometime else?

**Person 3:** Uh, right before I leave, hmmm I'll check it twice. Once when I wake up to know when I need to be ready. And then second when I'm about to go because usually I'll be late. So, I just need to re-make sure.

**Adiena:** Okay, so let's say you have the application, right? Do you want the application to only tell you yes or no? For example, yes, you will make it on time or no, you will not. Or do you want the application to provide more details?

**Person 3:** I want it to give me the exact amount of time that it will take me to get to the class just because sometimes I'm not planning to go right away. So, I feel like knowing just the time will make it easier to plan out my day.

**Adiena:** So, even in weeks where you already know your routes well, do you think something like this application would be usefu?  Or do you think it will only be useful at the start of the semester?

**Person 3:** I think UQ is quite a big place. Not really quite a big place, it's just there's a lot of buildings, and I feel like there's always a new building or a new place that I have to go to, maybe for meetings or non-class.

**Adiena:** Like tutorials?

**Person 3:** No, more like organisational meetings or events. So I feel like it would be helpful for that.

**Adiena:** Oh, so you can say that due to these organisational events, like outside class, it would make you want to open the app?

**Person 3:** Yeah, of course.
