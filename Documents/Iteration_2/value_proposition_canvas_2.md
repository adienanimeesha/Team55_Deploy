## Value Proposition Canvas (Iteration 2)

---

### VPC 1 — Segment 1: Students New to UQ Campus

*(First-year undergraduates, transfer students, and newly arrived international students)*

**Customer Jobs**

- Get to classes on time during the first weeks of semester, when buildings, rooms, and routes are still unfamiliar.

- Find the exact room inside a building — not just the building itself — including the correct floor, wing, and entrance, especially in multi-entry buildings where the wrong door leaves you on the wrong level.

- Move between consecutive classes within tight 10-minute cross-campus gaps, including when the previous class runs over its scheduled end time.

- Keep track of class schedules and assignment deadlines across multiple courses without manually cross-referencing Blackboard, my.UQ, Google Maps, and personal calendars.

- Build a reliable daily routine for moving around campus so that navigation becomes automatic rather than a daily planning task.

- Feel confident and independent when navigating the university environment, rather than relying on friends or strangers for directions, or "looking like a newbie" walking around inside an unfamiliar building.

- Continue to find unfamiliar rooms throughout the degree — for electives in new buildings, assessed practicals, lab rooms, organisational meetings, and library study sessions — not only in the first weeks.

- Access course materials, announcements, and submissions efficiently through a single platform rather than checking multiple sources.

Customer Jobs Hypotheses:

[Accepted] Finding the correct room inside a building is a higher-value job than finding the building itself, and is recalled across all year levels.

[Accepted] Tight cross-campus gaps are a real and recurring job, not just a first-week onboarding job.

[Refuted] All first-year students experience pre-departure anxiety as a daily job.

**Customer Pains**

- No single university platform integrates timetables, deadlines, and campus navigation, forcing students to switch between three or more tools (Blackboard, my.UQ, Google Maps, UQ Maps) for a single task.

- Google Maps gives indirect or longer routes on campus, does not recognise smaller building numbers, and stops at the building exterior — leaving students to figure out the inside themselves.

- UQ Maps, the existing campus-localised tool, is either unknown to students or known but rarely used because it is static — it gives a starting and ending point but does not move with the student during the walk.

- Estimated walk times do not account for finding the exact room, stairs, crowded paths, or going through buildings, so estimates run consistently long under real campus conditions.

- Multi-entry buildings (Hartley Teakle is a recurring named example) require students to know which entrance leads to which wing and which floor; entering on the wrong side forces an unnecessary lift trip and adds time the student did not budget.

- Specific recalled near-misses: an assessed practical almost missed because the student could not find the room across multiple levels; a library study session lost to walking every floor; a tutorial seat lost to arriving even slightly late.

- Many students do not know the Blackboard mobile app exists, so the university's primary academic platform never reaches them via push notifications. When notifications do arrive, they are perceived as poorly formatted and spammy.

- Anxiety and stress about being late to class, occurring both before departure and during the walk, especially for attendance-marked sessions or tutorials with limited seating.

- Having to rely on friends or strangers for directions, which creates dependency, undermines independence, and produces social embarrassment ("not wanting to look like a dumb person navigating the building like a newbie").

Customer Pain Hypotheses:

[Accepted] Google Maps inaccuracy on campus is a real, recallable, and specifically attributable pain.

[Accepted] The building-entrance cutoff is the moment existing tools stop being useful.

[Accepted] Multi-entry / wing-level confusion is a distinct, recalled sub-problem of indoor lostness.

[Accepted] Platform fragmentation is felt acutely enough that timetable auto-import is requested unprompted.

[Refuted] Inaccurate walking-time estimates are felt acutely by all students.

**Customer Gains**

- One integrated view showing what class, where it is, when to leave, and how to get there — eliminating the need to cross-reference multiple platforms.

- Confidence to navigate campus independently from the first day, both outside and inside buildings, without relying on friends or leaving excessively early as a buffer.

- Reduced first-week stress and a smoother transition into university life, targeting the period when navigation difficulty peaks.

- Specific, actionable information at decision time — leave-by time, exact arrival time, minutes-late indicator, and route on one screen — rather than a binary verdict that strips out the information students use to decide whether to push through, skip, or catch the recording.

- Avoidance of the social embarrassment of "looking like a newbie" wandering inside an unfamiliar building looking for a room.

- Less daily cognitive load spent on planning logistics like checking timetables, looking up locations, and estimating travel times.

- A tool that remains useful beyond the first weeks of semester — for electives in unfamiliar buildings, organisational meetings, library study sessions, and other non-classroom navigation across the degree.

Customer Gain Hypotheses:

[Accepted] Students value specific actionable information (leave-by time, arrival time, minutes-late, route) over a binary YES/NO verdict.

[Accepted] Reduced cognitive load is a real and named gain.

[Accepted] Tool relevance beyond first year is a real gain, anchored in indoor lostness and unfamiliar-room scenarios.

**Products & Services**

- An integrated class schedule view within Blackboard that displays timetable entries alongside building locations and room numbers, with the next-up class as the default landing view.

- Auto-imported timetable from the student's UQ account, eliminating manual building or room entry as the gateway friction to the tool.

- Direct navigation links from each timetable entry into UQ Maps' campus-specific routing, reusing UQ Maps' localised pathways rather than rebuilding them.

- Step-by-step indoor room-finding with landmark descriptions ("go up the stairs and turn right") — the priority MVP feature, preferred over abstract floor plans across all comparison interviews.

- Wing-and-entrance-aware routing that directs students to the correct entrance for their target floor, eliminating wrong-side entries that force unnecessary lift trips.

- Pre-departure planning view showing leave-by time as the most-actionable single number, paired with exact arrival time, a minutes-late indicator (when relevant), and the route on the same screen — no verdict-only mode.

- Live position-tracking navigation during the walk, addressing the static-navigation gap students experience with UQ Maps.

- Context-sensitive smart departure notification with a per-class on/off toggle and a one-tap silence / "stop navigating" control, defaulting to the progress-bar variant.

Products & Services Hypotheses:

[Accepted] Indoor room-finding is the priority MVP feature for this segment.

[Accepted] Auto-imported timetable is a desired feature, raised unprompted.

[Accepted] Localised campus routing using UQ Maps' shortcut data is a defensible adoption trigger.

[Refuted] A YES/NO verdict paired with a leave-by time is the right output for the pre-departure screen.

[Refuted] All students benefit from a single, always-on push notification.

[Partially Accepted] The pre-departure time scrubber is intuitive without instruction.

**Pain Relievers**

- Eliminates platform fragmentation by combining schedule, location, and navigation in one place within Blackboard.

- Surfaces UQ Maps' campus-specific routing automatically through timetable links, so students benefit from localised pathways without having to discover and adopt a separate app.

- Provides travel estimates that account for campus-specific factors beyond raw walking distance — internal pathways, stairs, room-finding time — reducing the gap between estimated and actual arrival time.

- Step-by-step indoor guidance addresses the building-entrance cutoff that Google Maps and UQ Maps share, eliminating the moment when navigation effectively "restarts" inside a building.

- Wing-and-entrance-aware routing eliminates the wrong-side-entry problem in multi-entry buildings.

- Live position-tracking during the walk addresses the static-navigation gap students experience with UQ Maps.

- Per-class notification controls eliminate the noise problem that makes some students reject reminders entirely.

- Delivers well-formatted, relevant departure reminders rather than generic notification spam, giving students a reason to download and keep the Blackboard app.

- Reduces pre-departure and mid-walk anxiety by providing clear, specific guidance — leave-by time, arrival time, and on-track status — instead of forcing students to estimate themselves.

- Supports independent navigation so students do not need to depend on friends or strangers for directions, including inside the building.

Pain Reliever Hypotheses:

[Accepted] Step-by-step indoor guidance with landmarks reduces indoor lostness more than a floor plan does.

[Accepted] Localised campus routing is a defensible reason for students to switch from Google Maps.

[Refuted] A single notification design works for all students.

**Gain Creators**

- Gives new students a single starting point for their daily campus routine from day one — no setup, no separate apps, no learning curve.

- Builds navigation confidence by providing campus-specific directions and room-level guidance that generic map apps cannot match — including inside the building, where Google Maps and UQ Maps both stop being useful.

- Provides specific, actionable times rather than verdicts, supporting the planning decisions students actually make ("push through and accept being two minutes late," "skip and catch the recording," "leave 5 minutes earlier next time").

- Reclaims time currently lost to excessive buffer margins by providing accurate, context-aware departure recommendations and on-track status during the walk.

- Reduces daily cognitive load by automating the "where, when, how long" calculation students currently perform manually across tools.

- Reusable beyond first year — the indoor room-finding, auto-imported timetable, and smart-notification features remain valuable for electives, labs, organisational meetings, and other unfamiliar-room scenarios across the degree.

- Creates a positive first impression of the university's digital support, signalling that UQ actively helps students settle in and continues to support them.

Gain Creator Hypotheses:

[Accepted] The tool extends beyond an onboarding-only use case.

[Accepted] Specific actionable times create more value than confidence-only gain framings.
