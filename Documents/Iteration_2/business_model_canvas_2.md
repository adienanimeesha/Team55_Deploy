## Business Model Canvas (Iteration 2)

**Customer Segments**

- Students new to UQ campus (first-years, transfer students, and newly arrived international students) who face navigation uncertainty, platform fragmentation, and lateness anxiety during their transition to university, especially in the first weeks of semester.

- All UQ undergraduate students managing cross-campus schedules, regardless of year level, who experience time-constraint stress when moving between back-to-back classes in different buildings.

Customer Segment Hypotheses:

[Accepted] First-year students experience significantly higher navigation anxiety than later-year students. 
(Evidence: Interviews showed first-years frequently get lost or arrive late in first few weeks).

[Accepted] Students with back-to-back classes across campus face severe time-constraint stress. 
(Evidence: Students reported 10-minute gaps are often insufficient for cross-campus travel, leading to consistent lateness).

[Refuted] University teaching staff will also use the app to navigate to new lecture halls. 
(Evidence: Interviews indicated staff largely rely on established routines and prefer their existing Outlook calendar setups).

**Value Propositions**

Reduce platform fragmentation by integrating class timetables, deadlines, and campus navigation into a single experience, replacing the current patchwork of Blackboard, my.UQ, Google Maps, and personal calendars.

Provide deadline-aware navigation that tells students not just how long a walk takes, but whether they will arrive on time given their departure time and pace — addressing the gap between raw ETAs and real campus conditions like stairs, room-finding, and route complexity.

Offer pre-departure planning guidance so students can assess when to leave and how much buffer time they need before starting their journey.

Provide in-transit pace feedback for students already walking, giving real-time reassurance about whether their current pace is sufficient to arrive on time.

Surface UQ Maps' localised campus features through integration with tools students already use, rather than expecting students to discover and adopt UQ Maps independently.

Value Proposition Hypotheses：

[Accepted] Students value "deadline-aware" navigation (knowing if they will be late) more than standard routing (just knowing the path). 
(Evidence: Interviewees expressed that raw ETAs from Google Maps do not account for finding the specific room inside a building).

[Accepted] Consolidating the timetable and navigation reduces cognitive load. 
(Evidence: Users strongly disliked having to manually cross-reference my.UQ timetables with map applications).

[Refuted] Students want social features integrated into the map (e.g., seeing where friends are). 
(Evidence: User feedback showed a strong preference for a strictly functional, utility-focused tool to get to class on time, viewing social features as unnecessary clutter).

**Channels**

Blackboard (Learn.UQ) integration via both the website and mobile app, since Blackboard is the one university tool every student already opens daily — though many students are unaware the app exists.

Context-sensitive push notifications via the Blackboard mobile app for departure reminders, designed to be specific and actionable rather than the generic, poorly formatted notifications students currently criticise.

Direct linking from timetable entries to UQ Maps for campus-specific navigation, eliminating the manual step of copying building names into Google Maps.
Word of mouth and peer recommendation.

Channel Hypotheses：

[Accepted] Integrating with Blackboard is the most reliable way to reach students daily. 
(Evidence: All interviewed students reported checking Blackboard multiple times a day for coursework).

[Refuted] Students will happily download a brand new, standalone scheduling app. 
(Evidence: App fatigue is high; users explicitly stated they do not want to download another university-specific app if they can avoid it).

**Customer Relationships**

Automated, self-service interaction where the system handles contextual work (calculating travel time, sending departure reminders) so students do not have to.

Personalised, context-sensitive communication — for example, actionable departure alerts tied to specific classes rather than generic course announcements.

Low-friction onboarding that surfaces the tool proactively during orientation and first-week communications, rather than requiring students to discover it on their own.

Customer Relationship Hypotheses:

[Accepted] Students prefer automated push notifications over having to manually check an app to know when to leave. (Evidence: Users reported forgetting to check their schedules until it was too late, making proactive reminders highly valuable).

[Accepted] Proactive onboarding during O-Week will significantly increase adoption. (Evidence: Upper-year students noted they only discovered useful UQ tools through word-of-mouth well into their first semester).

**Revenue Streams**

University-funded as part of student services infrastructure, since the product supports student engagement, retention, and on-time attendance.

No direct cost to students — the tool addresses a gap in the university's own digital ecosystem and should be treated as part of that infrastructure.

Potential licensing or partnership value if the solution proves effective and is adapted for other universities facing similar platform fragmentation.

Revenue Stream Hypotheses:

[Accepted] The product must be free for students to ensure widespread adoption. 
(Evidence: Students have zero willingness to pay for basic logistical university tools).

[Refuted] We can monetize via targeted on-campus vendor advertising (e.g., campus cafes). 
(Evidence: Early feedback suggested ads would damage trust and detract from the core value of reducing cognitive load and stress).
